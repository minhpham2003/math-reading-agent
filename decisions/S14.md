# S14 — A dense definition

**Design status:** Closed (reader approved 2026-11-01)  
**Implementation:** Not implemented or verified in this repository.

## Scenario and boundary

> You reach a definition whose formal statement is short but conceptually dense. You need motivation, examples, and enough space to think about it yourself.

This scenario addresses support for a difficult definition. Ordinary reader-driven reading and discussion follow S12.

## Approved decisions

### S14-D01 — Passive awareness of the current PDF page
**Status:** Approved (2026-11-01).  
**Decision: Current-page awareness without interruption.** The PDF viewer should make the currently viewed page available as context to the agent while the reader reads silently. This is contextual information, **not** a license to interrupt, assume which definition the reader is examining, or infer completion. The agent also knows the assigned passage from the reading plan.  
**Alternatives discussed:** Knowing only the assigned reading; tracking the viewed PDF page; knowing the exact paragraph or definition automatically.  
**Rationale / approval evidence:** The reader clarified that the agent is not continuously interacting while they read and then explicitly said, “yes knowing the page is useful.”

### S14-D02 — Natural reference plus optional PDF selection
**Status:** Approved (2026-11-01).  
**Decision: Option C — Both natural references and PDF highlighting.** The reader may ask about a definition conversationally, with the agent using the currently viewed page and reading context to interpret the reference. The reader may also select/highlight a precise passage in the PDF to attach it as the question's context. If the reference remains ambiguous, the agent asks which definition is meant rather than guessing.  
**Alternatives discussed:** Always ask which definition; require PDF selection; support both.  
**Rationale / approval evidence:** Reader explicitly selected C.

### S14-D03 — Context-sensitive conversational support
**Status:** Approved (2026-11-01).  
**Decision: Option C — Respond conversationally.** When the reader asks for help with a dense definition, the agent considers the specific question, the definition, and relevant previous discussion. It may ask for clarification, invite the reader's own interpretation, or offer appropriately scoped help depending on context. Preserve the established preference not to volunteer key mathematical insights, hints, or solutions without invitation; selecting contextual behavior does not itself authorize unsolicited explanations.
**Alternatives discussed:** Always ask what is confusing; always present a menu of approaches; contextual conversation.
**Rationale / approval evidence:** Reader explicitly selected Option C.

### S14-D04 — Source-first contextual guidance and clickable passages
**Status:** Approved (2026-11-01).  
**Decision: Option B — Clickable references to earlier material.** When a dense definition raises questions, prioritize relevant earlier definitions, examples, constructions, or arguments in the **current source**, including the author's own motivational development. Invite the reader to investigate connections rather than prematurely explaining them. Provide a clickable PDF reference to the earlier passage, preserving an easy return to the original definition. Other sources can remain available but are secondary by default.  
**Alternatives discussed:** Mention earlier material without navigation; clickable navigation; automatically display the earlier passage.  
**Rationale / approval evidence:** Reader requested examples from the uploaded cohomology chapter, suggested recalling analogous constructions, explicitly prioritized the current source, and selected B for clickable references.

### S14-D05 — Progressive guidance
**Status:** Approved (2026-11-01).  
**Decision: Option B — Progressive hints.** When the reader remains uncertain, begin with minimal assistance and increase the specificity of hints or explanations in response to requests and demonstrated need, rather than following a rigid hint ladder. Do not give away key mathematical insights unnecessarily; preserve room for independent thinking.  
**Alternatives discussed:** Continue only with questions; progressive hints; ask permission before each new mathematical insight.  
**Rationale / approval evidence:** Reader explicitly selected B.

### S14-D06 — Adaptive understanding checks
**Status:** Approved (2026-11-01).  
**Decision: Option C — Context-sensitive verification for important definitions.** A statement such as “I understand now” does not automatically trigger a quiz. Consider a short reader-generated explanation or example when the definition is particularly foundational and understanding has not already been demonstrated. Weigh mathematical significance, available evidence of understanding, and whether checking would be disruptive.  
**Alternatives discussed:** Accept understanding without a check; always request demonstration; context-dependent check.  
**Rationale / approval evidence:** Reader chose C and added that particularly important definitions may warrant asking for a demonstration of understanding.

### S14-D07 — Socratic correction
**Status:** Approved (2026-11-01).  
**Decision: Option B — Socratic correction.** When the reader makes a subtle conceptual error about a definition, ask a targeted question that helps them recognize and repair the error themselves rather than immediately giving the correction. Avoid repetitive unproductive questioning; retain the reader's control of the mathematical argument.  
**Alternatives discussed:** Immediate correction; Socratic questioning; adaptive direct correction.  
**Rationale / approval evidence:** Reader explicitly selected B.

### S14-D08 — No automatic discussion closure
**Status:** Approved (2026-11-01).  
**Decision: No automatic discussion closure — Reader-driven rhythm.** The agent does not infer understanding or completion from silence, declare a definition finished, or interrupt independent reading. It remains available; the reader can continue, return with another question, or resume the assigned passage naturally. Awareness of the current PDF page is not awareness of the reader's exact intellectual activity.  
**Alternatives discussed:** Suggest return to reading; explicitly wait for a resumption signal; adaptive suggestions; uninterrupted reader-led interaction.  
**Rationale / approval evidence:** Reader emphasized that they are usually “doing my thing” and that the agent cannot know exactly what they are doing; then explicitly confirmed this formulation.

## Pending questions

- Exact eye gaze or word/paragraph tracking is **not** assumed or required; no such feature was designed.
- Detailed navigation/back-stack interaction beyond a clickable reference and easy return may be specified in the PDF navigation scenario.

## Approved reader experience and closure (2026-11-01)

The reader explores a dense definition independently while the agent remains quiet, aware only of assigned reading and PDF page context. Questions can be asked naturally or anchored with a PDF selection. The agent responds conversationally and source-first, linking back to earlier passages and inviting the reader to discover connections. Assistance can become progressively more explicit if needed, while misconceptions are addressed first through Socratic questions. Understanding checks are selective, especially for foundational definitions; silence never means completion. The reader controls when to continue.

**Design closure:** Reader confirmed S14 is ready to close (“should be good”).  
**Implementation / deployment / verification:** Documentation design only; no S14 feature implementation, deployed flow, browser test, or verification is claimed.  
**Deferred:** Detailed PDF navigation behavior; exact eye/word tracking not specified.

## Relationship to S12

S12-D02 requires silence and no automatic check-ins during assigned reading. S14-D01 preserves that agreement.
