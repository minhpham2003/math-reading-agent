# S15 — A long proof

**Design status:** Closed (reader approved 2026-11-01)
**Implementation:** Not implemented, deployed, tested, or verified in this repository.

## Original scenario
> You reach a substantial proof. Some parts matter conceptually while others are technical. You want the agent to guide attention without replacing the act of reading the proof.

## Approved decisions

### S15-D01 — Option B: Structural roadmap before reading
**Status:** Approved. **Decision:** The agent offers a compact high-level map of the proof's stages before independent reading, without explaining the crucial arguments. **Alternatives:** start reading without guidance; reconstruct only after reading. **Rationale/evidence:** Reader selected B.

### S15-D02 — Option C: Expandable roadmap
**Status:** Approved; supersedes initial selection of Option B. **Decision:** The roadmap initially lists logical stages, corresponding PDF locations, and conceptual/technical roles. Clicking a stage expands a 1–2 sentence explanation of what it accomplishes, without giving away its argument; stages can be collapsed independently. **Alternatives:** minimal overview; permanently visible explanations. **Rationale/evidence:** Reader clarified that clickable expansion was intended and changed their choice to C.

### S15-D03 — Option C: Independent reading
**Status:** Approved. **Decision:** Reader reads the entire proof independently with roadmap available; no mandatory stage checkpoints or interruptions. **Alternatives:** mandatory checkpoints; optional stage-by-stage check-ins. **Rationale/evidence:** Reader selected C.

### S15-D04 — Option C, revised presentation: Conversational discussion suggestions
**Status:** Approved, with later interface clarification. **Decision:** After reading, the agent may offer three avenues in natural text: reconstruct the proof together; ask a specific question; check a reader-authored explanation. These are conversational suggestions, NOT required menu buttons or modes, and the reader may respond freely. **Alternatives:** agent starts reconstruction; unrestricted open prompt; mandatory three-option menu (superseded in presentation). **Rationale/evidence:** Reader selected C, then clarified the agent should suggest options through text, not micromanage with repeated menus.

### S15-D05 — Option C: Flexible skipping
**Status:** Approved. **Decision:** Reader may skip a proof entirely, request a big-picture account, or focus on conceptually essential parts instead of technical details. These choices may be communicated conversationally rather than through a mandatory selector. **Alternatives:** skip without comment; automatic takeaway only. **Rationale/evidence:** Reader selected C and emphasized that proofs may be too technical, niche, or long for current needs.

### S15-D06 — Option A: Return fresh
**Status:** Approved. **Decision:** Returning to a skipped proof displays its ordinary roadmap, with no special skipped-proof reminder or forced resumption flow. **Alternatives:** resume with prior skip context; contextual menu of approaches. **Rationale/evidence:** Reader selected A.

### S15-D10 (joint with S18) — Option A: Silence while reading
**Status:** Approved. **Decision:** Once reading begins, agent stays silent unless reader initiates interaction; no time-based check-ins or completion inference. **Alternatives:** occasional check-ins; conditional interventions. **Rationale/evidence:** Reader selected A; consistent with S14 and S12.

## Approved reader experience and closure
A concise interactive roadmap helps orient attention; reader expands stages as desired, reads without interruption, may choose a lighter engagement with the proof, and initiates conversational discussion when useful. The agent is a high-level coordinator, not a rigid workflow manager. Recommendations about what deserves attention belong to S18 and occur at section entry, not repeated during the proof. Reader accepted the combined S15/S18 walk-through with “should be good” (2026-11-01).

## Boundaries / deferrals
General goal-aware material triage is S18. Dense definitions remain S14; substantial proof detours remain S22. Proof roadmap visual interaction is distinct from compulsory choice menus. No claim of app feature implementation or real-PDF behavior.
